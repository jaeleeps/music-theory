# Sources

A vetted list of real references. When the student asks for further reading, or you want to point to a source, recommend from this list or name a source you are certain exists. Never make up a title, author, edition, or URL. Free and openly licensed sources are listed first, so the student can open them right away.

## 2. Notation references

- Elaine Gould, *Behind Bars: The Definitive Guide to Music Notation* (Faber Music, 2011). The standard professional reference on how notation should look and be laid out. Cite it for engraving and notation-practice questions.
- [Standard Music Font Layout (SMuFL)](https://www.w3.org/2021/03/smufl14/), version 1.4, W3C Music Notation Community Group (2021). A catalog of about 2,400 notation symbols in 118 groups, each with an official name. Use it to identify unusual symbols and their names.
- [List of musical symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_musical_symbols) (Wikipedia, CC BY-SA). A quick, illustrated overview.
- [Musical Symbols](https://www.tampacomposer.com/pdf/musical-symbols-simple.pdf) (tampacomposer.com, PDF). A beginner's illustrated glossary, used as a coverage checklist for this skill. It has errors: it says a double sharp raises a note "by an entire semitone" (it's two semitones), that *mf* is "half as loud as forte", and that staccato is always exactly half the note value. Don't use it as an authority.

## 3. ABC notation and the renderer

- [ABC notation standard v2.1](https://abcnotation.com/wiki/abc:standard:v2.1). The format specification.
- [abcjs documentation](https://docs.abcjs.net/), and the [abcjs source](https://github.com/paulrosen/abcjs) (MIT). abcjs 6.7.1 is the version `assets/score.html` loads. Its decoration list (`legalAccents`, `volumeDecorations`, `dynamicDecorations` in the source) is the authority on which `!…!` marks render. abc-notation.md lists what has been tested.

## 4. Piano pedagogy

- [The Frances Clark Center](https://pianoinspires.com/) (Piano Inspires): publishes *The Piano Magazine* and the *Journal of Piano Research*, runs NCKP: The Piano Conference, and offers teacher-education programs.
- Michael Clark, [*Piano Basics*](https://openbooks.library.baylor.edu/pianobasics) (Baylor University open book). A free class-piano text for the first two semesters for music majors.
- Athina Fytika, [*Piano Pedagogy*](https://repository.kallipos.gr/handle/11419/12938) (Kallipos, Greek open academic repository). A university textbook for teaching beginner pianists.
- Print standards (verify the edition the student's course uses): Marienne Uszler, Stewart Gordon & Scott McBride Smith, *The Well-Tempered Keyboard Teacher*. Jeanine M. Jacobson, *Professional Piano Teaching*. Martha Baker-Jordan, *Practical Piano Pedagogy*. Jane Magrath, *The Pianist's Guide to Standard Teaching and Performance Literature*.

## 5. Scores and practice tools

- [IMSLP / Petrucci Music Library](https://imslp.org/): public-domain scores. Point the student here for full scores of the repertoire you discuss.
- [musictheory.net](https://www.musictheory.net/): free lessons and drills (intervals, chords, key signatures, ear training).

## 6. Standard print textbooks

These are the textbooks a course is most likely to use (see theory.md section 1): Aldwell, Schachter & Cadwallader, *Harmony and Voice Leading*. Kostka, Payne & Almén, *Tonal Harmony*. Laitz, *The Complete Musician*. Clendinning & Marvin, *The Musician's Guide to Theory and Analysis*. Caplin, *Classical Form* and *Analyzing Classical Form*. Hepokoski & Darcy, *Elements of Sonata Theory*. Fux, *Gradus ad Parnassum* (trans. Alfred Mann, *The Study of Counterpoint*). Recommend the edition the student's course uses, and don't quote page numbers.
